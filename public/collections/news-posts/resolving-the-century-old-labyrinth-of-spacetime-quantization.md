In the preceding argument, the metaphysical concept of Einstein's curved spacetime was dismantled. Consequently, an alternative framework for spacetime must take its place.



Every single physical experiment we conduct is measured against a scale of time and length defined by assuming a single, specific inertial frame—namely, the Earth. All physical phenomena can be fully measured and described using this universal metric. Applying the time and space defined within this single inertial frame to all other reference frames corresponds precisely to a spacetime governed by Galilean transformations.



Under Galilean transformations, any physical equation established in one reference frame automatically holds true across all other inertial frames because the transformation mathematically reduces every moving frame into a stationary one. Therefore, there is absolutely no necessity for physical equations to be formulated in a covariantly transformed (general covariant) manner.



However, we must still define the operational laws governing how light emitted from an external, moving system is observed within our stationary frame. That specific observational translation rule is precisely what the Lorentz transformation represents. Einstein's General Relativity, then, is properly understood as a predictive theory describing how electromagnetic waves bend—that is, undergo a localized redshift—around mass and energy. As previously established, these numerical predictions align flawlessly with empirical data.



Thus, we can accurately predict the outcomes of all physical experiments without inventing the artificial concept of spacetime curvature. Time and space themselves are not the objects of measurement in physics; rather, it is the spatial domain and the duration (the physical processes) within them that we measure. Consequently, the quantization of spacetime itself is entirely unnecessary. Quantizing the intrinsic frequencies and wavenumbers that define those phenomena is perfectly necessary and sufficient.
